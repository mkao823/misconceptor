# Misconceptor: Project Review (2026-10-18)

*An outside review of the repository as of commit 2580c5c: what exists, what was
verified, where the gaps are, what to focus on next, and which additional
features or solutions are worth considering. Written by Claude at Michael's
request. Nothing in the repo was changed except adding this file.*

## 1. Verdict in brief

The thinking is in good shape. The research plan is unusually honest for a
course project: one mechanism claim, an ablation ladder with a compute-matched
control, a positive control, a shortcut-learning check, and a reportable null
result. The feasibility pilot did exactly what a pilot should: it found that the
original dataset (JorGPT) has zero in-scope questions and located a fallback.

The project is now blocked on three things, in this order:

1. **Taxonomy and data do not overlap enough.** The recursion inventory the
   plan relies on (Hamouda et al. 2017) catalogues misconceptions about *tracing
   and writing* recursive code (backward flow, the `return` keyword, base case
   placement). Mohler's in-scope questions are *recall* questions ("what causes
   infinite recursion?", "how many comparisons in a BST?"). Only two or three of
   the twelve recursion items can ever appear in Mohler answers. Macro-F1 over
   a twelve-plus-class label set is not computable on this data.
2. **The misconception-bearing pool is about 43 answers**, spread over 7
   questions, and many of the wrong answers are wrong facts or answers to a
   different question rather than catalogued misconceptions. The label set needs
   an `other_error` class, and the eval set needs a supplement.
3. **No code exists past the pilot.** No taxonomy file, no annotation schema, no
   model chosen, no LLM SDK installed, no retrieval corpus for condition 2, no
   specification of what the Critic judges or re-retrieves.

None of these is fatal. Section 5 gives a concrete order of work. The single
most important next step is a one-week annotation pilot on the 43 low-scoring
Mohler answers, because its outcome decides the data plan.

### 2.3 Data (`data/raw/`, gitignored)

- **JorGPT**: 3,041 rows, 50 questions, EN and ES. Confirmed out of scope.
- **Mohler ASAG**, `cleaned/open_ended` split from Hugging Face: 2,273 answers,
  81 questions, columns `id, question, instructor_answer, student_answer,
  score_grader_1, score_grader_2, score_avg`.

Two data facts the docs do not yet record:

- **Grading scale differs by exam.** Exams E01 to E10 are scored 0 to 5; E11 and
  E12 are scored 0 to 10 by each grader, and `score_avg` is rescaled to 0 to 5.
  I confirmed this from the per-exam maxima and from the dataset card. Use
  `score_avg` only, or rescale the grader columns before any threshold.
- **The `cleaned` split is Penn-Treebank tokenized.** Parentheses are `-LRB-`
  and `-RRB-`, "C++" is "C plus plus", exponents are "n to the power of 2",
  sentences end in `<STOP>`. This text will look strange to an LLM and will
  break any regex baseline. The dataset card also offers a `raw` config with the
  original, untokenized answers. Switch to `raw` for everything downstream, or
  add a normalization step.

### 2.4 What the in-scope Mohler answers actually contain

I read every answer to the seven strong and medium questions, sorted by grade.

| Question | n | score < 3.5 | score <= 2.5 | What the low scorers look like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E03.Q06 infinite recursion causes | 31 | 2 | 1 | Almost all correct. Little diagnostic signal. |
| E05.Q04 base case of merge sort | 28 | 7 | 7 | Five answer "O(n log n)", one pastes code, one is vague. These are not base-case misconceptions; they are answers to a different question. |
| E05.Q03 insertion sort best case | 28 | 8 | 4 | Real signal: swapped best/worst, "best case is 1 operation", "worst case N factorial", "n(n-1)/2 in the best case". |
| E10.Q07 BST comparisons | 24 | 8 | 7 | Real signal: "n-1", "n/2", "n log n", "at most 3", "(n^k)-1". |
| E12.Q10 BST search steps | 28 | 9 | 9 | Real signal: "n/2", "2n-1", "2^n", "n(n-1)/2", "3 steps, there are 3 cases". |
| E03.Q07 iteration vs recursion | 31 | 6 | 3 | Mostly incomplete rather than wrong. |
| E11.Q09 divide and conquer | 30 | 3 | 0 | Confuses it with modular decomposition into files or classes. Interesting but not in any catalogue. |
| **Total** | **200** | **43** | **31** | |

Reading this, the Big-O side has the better misconception signal (BST search
confused with linear search or with sorting; best case confused with smallest
input; best and worst case swapped). The recursion side is mostly correct
recall or off-topic answers. This inverts the plan's emphasis, which leans on
the recursion inventory.

## 3. What I verified against sources

The repo's own discipline is to mark unverified claims, so here is what I
checked today.

### 3.1 The "92-item DSA systematic review" exists

The research plan could not find it. It is Tilanterä and Korhonen, "Data
structures and algorithms misconceptions in concept inventories: a systematic
literature review," *Computer Science Education*, 2026,
[doi:10.1080/08993408.2026.2633989](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08993408.2026.2633989).
Search snippets say it reviewed seven DSA concept inventories across nine
reports and extracted 92 misconceptions or difficulties, mapped onto the CS
Curricula 2023 knowledge areas. The publisher blocked my fetch, so the C1/C2
category codes and the per-category counts are still unverified; get the PDF
and the supplementary catalogue through the SJSU library. Tilanterä's earlier
[doctoral dissertation at Aalto](https://aaltodoc3.org.aalto.fi/items/d81f9b00-9674-4d2d-a1fe-ca6a9fd2f7c0)
describes a 70-item catalogue covering DSA subtopics and recursion, so the two
numbers refer to different versions.

### 3.2 The recursion inventory's actual item list

I extracted the text of Hamouda, Edwards, Elmongui, Ernst and Shaffer 2017
([open PDF on Shaffer's site](https://people.cs.vt.edu/~shaffer/Papers/RecurTutorCIPublished.pdf)).
The misconceptions came from about 8,000 exam responses in a CS2 course over
three semesters plus interviews. The paper gives each item a tag. Verbatim
tags, grouped as in the paper:

| Group | Tag | Item (paraphrased) | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backward flow | `BFneverExecute` | No statements after the recursive call execute | Misconception |
| Backward flow | `BFexecuteBefore` | Statements after the recursive call execute before it | Misconception |
| Infinite recursion | `InfiniteExecution` | A base case always executes even if the call does not reduce the problem | Misconception |
| Recursive call | `RCwrite` | Cannot write a call that reaches the base case | Difficulty |
| Recursive call | `RCnoReturnRequired` | A value is returned even if `return` is omitted | Misconception |
| Recursive call | `RCreturnIsRequired` | All recursive calls need `return` even for void functions | Misconception |
| Base case | `BCbeforeRecursiveCase` | Base case must appear before the recursive call, in the `if` | Misconception |
| Base case | `BCactionReturnConstant` | Base case must return a constant | Misconception |
| Base case | `BCcheckAganistConstant` | Base case must compare a variable to a constant | Misconception |
| Base case | `BCwrite` | Cannot write a correct base case | Difficulty |
| Base case | `BCevaluation` | Off-by-one in how many times the method runs | Difficulty |
| Updating variables | `GlobalVariable` | A "global" initialized inside the function persists across calls | Misconception |

Consequences for the repo:

- The research plan's example `MR1 = "Recursive call requires a return"` is
  the right idea with the wrong code. The paper's tag is `RCreturnIsRequired`.
  Replace it.
- Nine of the twelve items require seeing the student reason about *code*. A
  recall answer such as "no base case" can only evidence `InfiniteExecution`
  (and only weakly), `BCwrite`, or `RCwrite`. This is the taxonomy-data gap in
  section 1.
- The paper reports prevalence per item (for example `BCwrite` 42 percent,
  `BFneverExecute` 28 percent in the second administration). Those numbers are
  useful for arguing which items matter.

### 3.3 Big-O taxonomy sources

- Farghally, Koh, Ernst and Shaffer, "Towards a concept inventory for
  algorithm analysis topics," SIGCSE 2017, pp. 207 to 212. Confirmed to exist
  via citing papers; the item list is behind the ACM paywall. The same group
  built an Algorithm Analysis Concept Inventory via a Delphi process
  ([VTechWorks record](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/73539)),
  and Shaffer hosts an asymptotic-analysis misconception practice battery on
  the OpenDSA/SPLICE catalogue
  ([catalog item](https://splice.cs.vt.edu/catalog/item/13208), contents not
  inspected).
- Danielsiek, Paul and Vahrenhold 2012
  ([doi:10.1145/2157136.2157148](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/2157136.2157148))
  covers algorithm analysis and complexity among its areas.
- The Mohler low scorers in section 2.4 suggest at least four Big-O item
  candidates that are likely in these sources: BST search confused with linear
  scan (answers of n, n-1, n/2); search confused with sort (n log n); "best
  case" confused with "smallest input" (1 operation, two elements); best and
  worst case swapped. Confirm the wording against Farghally before using.

### 3.4 Closest prior work the survey is missing

- **Oli, Banjade, Olney and Rus, "Can LLMs Identify Gaps and Misconceptions
  in Students' Code Explanations?"**
  ([arXiv:2501.10365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10365)). The nearest thing to
  this project in CS: free-text explanations of CS1/CS2 code (loops, recursion,
  sorting), GPT-4 vs LLaMA3 vs Mistral, zero-shot vs SFT vs ORPO. Two findings
  matter here. Their "student" data is *simulated* (2,888 explanations made by
  deleting sentences or injecting errors into expert explanations), which is a
  precedent for a synthetic supplement. And models flagged non-existent
  problems in 27 percent of correct explanations and 34 percent of
  typo-only ones, which is exactly the `none`-class precision risk in this
  project.
- **Zhao, Jiao and Xu, "Beyond Score Accuracy"**
  ([arXiv:2610.09460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09460), October 2026).
  Confirmed: it analyzes the JorGPT data. Its headline is that LLM feedback
  identified student misconceptions in 5 to 7 percent of responses versus 15 to
  31 percent for teachers, and that rubric sub-scores are redundant
  (r = 0.82 to 0.99). This is the best single motivation citation for the
  problem statement and should lead the related-work section.
- **McMining** ([arXiv:2510.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08827)):
  discovering misconceptions from student *code*; releases a benchmark.
- **Yeltekin and Bauer, "Can LLMs Simulate Novice Programmers'
  Misconceptions?"** ([arXiv:2610.10656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10656)):
  13 LLMs on generating, solving, simulating and diagnosing misconceptions in
  code-tracing problems; diagnosis is easier in true/false format than
  open-ended. Relevant to designing synthetic data and to expectations for
  small local models.
- **ProgMiscon** ([progmiscon.org](https://progmiscon.org/misconceptions/)):
  104 catalogued programming misconceptions. Five are recursion-related
  (`BaseCaseNotNeeded`, `RecursiveActivationsShareFrame`,
  `RecursiveMethodImpliesRecursiveType`, `RecursiveMethodNeedsIfElse`,
  `RecursiveFunctionNeedsIfElse`); none cover Big-O. A second published source
  for the recursion side.
- **Recursion mental models**: Kahney 1983 (copies vs loop model); Götschi,
  Sanders and Galpin 2003
  ([page](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/vgalpin1/pubs/GSG03.html)) and
  Sanders, Galpin and Götschi 2006
  ([page](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/vgalpin1/pubs/SGG06.html)): copies,
  looping, active, step, magic, return-value and odd models. These are
  *mental-model* categories rather than item-level misconceptions, and they
  are the natural labels for conceptual (non-code) answers like Mohler's.
  Haberman and Averbuch 2002, "The case of base cases," adds base-case
  difficulties.
- **Korthals et al. 2026** is already in the survey; note it also motivates
  an alternative Critic trigger (section 6.6).

### 3.5 No public dataset with recursion or Big-O misconception labels

I searched for one and found none. The closest are Eedi (math, coarse labels),
the AP Biology short-answer misconception set, MAP-Charting (math), the Hamburg
algorithms code dataset (no misconception labels), and Oli et al.'s simulated
set (available on request). The 2026 misconception-diagnosis paper already in
the survey says the same. Building the eval set is unavoidable, and the repo
already accepts that.

## 4. Gaps, ranked

**G1. Taxonomy versus data mismatch (critical).** See sections 1 and 3.2. The
label set the system predicts must be restricted to items the eval data can
exhibit, and the write-up must say which taxonomy items were out of reach for
these question types. Otherwise per-class F1 has many empty classes.

**G2. Eval set size and composition (critical).** About 43 misconception
candidates, 7 questions, and a majority of errors that are "wrong fact" or
"answered a different question." Add an `other_error` label (wrong, but not a
catalogued misconception) distinct from `none` and `insufficient_evidence`.
Without it, annotators will force answers into the nearest taxonomy item and
inflate agreement on the wrong thing. Also note that the 43 are clustered in
7 questions, so a bootstrap over responses understates variance; report that
as a limitation.

**G3. No taxonomy file, no annotation schema, no second rater (high).** The
team is one person. Cohen's kappa needs a second human. Identify a classmate
or the course TA now and budget them 30 to 50 items.

**G4. Condition 2 has no corpus (high).** "Course-material RAG" needs course
material. Mohler ships only one-line instructor answers. Options in section
6.2.

**G5. The Critic is unspecified (high).** What does "insufficient" mean
operationally? What pool is re-retrieved from, with what query, how many
rounds, and what stops the loop? The positive control (withheld chunk) can only
be built once the pool is chunked and indexed. Write a one-page spec before
coding.

**G6. No model, no SDK, no cost estimate (high).** `default_model: TODO`.
Every condition must share one base model; pick it this week.

**G7. Sensitivity of the experiment (high, easy to miss).** Conditions 2, 3
and 4 can only differ if, for some eval items, retrieved content changes the
right label. For ten-word recall answers, the limiting factor is the answer's
information content, not retrieval. One Mohler example where retrieval could
matter: the merge sort base case is "1 (or 2)" in this course's instructor
answer, so an answer of "two elements" is correct *here* and a course-specific
chunk resolves that. Design a "retrieval-dependent" subset of eval items on
purpose, or the mechanism claim is untestable by construction.

**G8. Contamination and supplement (medium).** Mohler is from 2009 to 2011 and
widely used. The plan's mitigation (compare with self-collected answers) has
no collection plan and an open IRB question. Section 6.3 gives alternatives.

**G9. Text normalization (medium, cheap).** Use the `raw` Mohler config;
rescale grader columns; fix the Big-O regex for the tokenized form if `cleaned`
is kept.

**G10. Engineering scaffolding (medium).** No tests, no results schema, no
prompt versioning, no LLM-call cache, no run manifest. Bootstrap CIs and 4b
sampling mean many calls; a cache keyed on (model, prompt, temperature, seed)
saves money and makes reruns deterministic.

**G11. Literature survey holes (medium).** Areas 3 and 4 empty; Area 2 missing
the items in 3.4; Area 1 missing the ASAG lineage (Mohler et al. 2011 itself;
Burrows, Gurevych and Stein 2015, "The eras and trends of automatic short
answer grading," which I recall but have not verified today).

**G12. Timeline (medium).** Still TODO. Assuming the SJSU fall semester ends in
mid-December, about eight weeks remain. Section 5 is sized for that.

**G13. Small doc inconsistencies (low).** README says `data/raw/` holds "JorGPT
CSVs"; it now also holds Mohler. `.env.example` has a placeholder key name.
`taxonomy/recursion_bigO.yaml` is referenced by the proposal but does not
exist.

## 5. Where to focus, in order

Sized for roughly eight weeks, one person, with the annotation pilot as the
decision point.

**Week 1: taxonomy and annotation pilot (decides everything else).**

1. Write `taxonomy/recursion_bigO.yaml` with the twelve Hamouda tags, the five
   ProgMiscon recursion items, the Götschi mental-model categories, and the
   Big-O candidates from 3.3, each with `id`, `source`, `description`,
   `observable_in` (code-tracing, code-writing, conceptual recall) and
   `example_trigger_phrases` (for condition 0). Mark unverified Big-O items as
   provisional until Farghally and Tilanterä are read.
2. Write `annotation/schema.md`: label set is taxonomy IDs plus `none`,
   `other_error`, `insufficient_evidence`; one primary label, optional
   secondary; evidence span required for any taxonomy label; decision rules
   for the common confusions seen in 2.4.
3. Annotate the 43 low scorers plus 20 high scorers (to populate `none`).
   Record how many items landed in `other_error`. If more than half do, the
   Mohler-only plan is dead and step 4 becomes mandatory.
4. Decide the supplement (section 6.3) based on step 3.

**Week 2: model, harness, condition 1 smoke test.**

5. Pick the base model. Recommendation in 6.1. Install the SDK; fill
   `configs/models.yaml`.
6. Build the shared harness: prompt templates with version strings, JSON
   output schema with a validator, LLM-call cache, `results/<run_id>/` with
   config snapshot, raw outputs and `metrics.json`. Unit tests for macro-F1,
   bootstrap and kappa against hand-computed examples.
7. Run condition 1 on the pilot cases. The plan already says this answers
   "is the ladder worth building." Also run condition 0.

**Weeks 3 to 4: corpus, conditions 2 and 3, retrieval-dependent items.**

8. Assemble the retrieval pool (6.2), chunk it, index it. Tag which eval
   items are retrieval-dependent (G7).
9. Conditions 2 and 3. Condition 3 is the real baseline; get its number
   first, with CIs, before building anything agentic.

**Weeks 5 to 6: condition 4, 4b, controls.**

10. Critic spec (6.5), then condition 4. Then 4b with the token budget matched
    to 4's measured mean.
11. Positive control (withheld chunk) and paraphrase control. Grounding-support
    ratings on a stratified sample.

**Weeks 7 to 8: write-up.** Per-class F1, confusion matrices, paired bootstrap
on 4 minus 3 and 4 minus 4b, cost table, limitations (clustering by question,
contamination, synthetic supplement if used).

If time runs short, cut from the bottom: the paraphrase control, then
grounding-support human ratings (keep the automatic citation-validity check in
6.4), then 4b. Do not cut condition 3 or the annotation pilot.

## 6. Features and solutions worth considering

### 6.1 Local-first base model

Ollama is already installed with `gpt-oss:20b`. Serving it through Ollama's
OpenAI-compatible endpoint fits the existing `base_url` field in
`configs/models.yaml`. Benefits: zero marginal cost for the thousands of calls
that bootstrap and 4b imply; full reproducibility with a fixed seed; no key
management. Risk: a 20B model may be too weak for condition 1 to "get anywhere
close," which would make the ladder uninformative. Mitigation: run the
condition 1 smoke test on both the local model and one API model, then commit
to one for the ladder and keep the other as a robustness check in the appendix.
Whatever is chosen, use the same model for every condition, including the
Critic.

### 6.2 A retrieval corpus for condition 2 that can actually move the needle

Three options, cheapest first:

- **Write your own course notes.** Two to three pages per concept (recursion,
  base cases, call stack; asymptotic notation, best/average/worst case, BST
  search cost, sorting costs), written as an instructor would, including the
  course's conventions (for example "merge sort base case is size 1 or 2",
  "count comparisons, not swaps"). This is legitimately "the instructor's
  course material," it is small enough to chunk cleanly, and you control
  which facts exist so the withheld-chunk control is easy to build.
- **OpenDSA chapters** on recursion and algorithm analysis (Creative Commons,
  from the same Virginia Tech group as the inventories). More realistic, more
  noise.
- **Mohler's own instructor answers** as a minimal "course material" set.
  Too thin on its own but should be included.

Whatever the pool, build the retrieval-dependent subset of eval items
deliberately (G7).

### 6.3 Supplementing the eval set without an IRB delay

- **Synthetic misconception-bearing answers, human-verified.** For each
  taxonomy item, prompt a model *other than the base model* to write short
  student-style answers to the Mohler questions and to new code-tracing
  questions modelled on Hamouda's, exhibiting that item. A human (you) then
  keeps only answers that genuinely exhibit the item and reads naturally.
  Precedent: Oli et al. and Yeltekin and Bauer. Report Mohler and synthetic
  results separately; never pool them silently. This also produces the
  paraphrase-control and withheld-evidence items.
- **New code-tracing questions.** Hamouda's items are observable only when a
  student predicts output or writes code. Writing six to eight such questions
  and collecting answers from classmates informally may fall under an exempt
  category, but check SJSU IRB before collecting anything from people.
- **Re-scope the recursion side.** If neither of the above is feasible, make
  Big-O the primary concept (it has the better natural signal in Mohler) and
  keep recursion limited to the two or three items Mohler can show. Say so in
  the report; it is a finding, not a failure.

### 6.4 Two grounding metrics, one automatic

Before the human "supports / partially / unrelated" rating, add an automatic
**citation validity** check: does each cited span exist verbatim (after
normalization) in the student answer or in a retrieved chunk? It is objective,
free, and catches fabricated evidence, which is the failure mode Oli et al.
report. Report both: validity rate (automatic, all items) and support rating
(human, sample).

### 6.5 Critic specification (one page, before coding)

Define: the retrieval pool and chunk size; the first-pass query (question plus
answer); the sufficiency prompt and its output schema (`sufficient: bool`,
`missing: str`); the re-query construction from `missing`; a maximum of two
refinement rounds; the stop rule; and what is logged per round (chunks
retrieved, sufficiency verdict, tokens). The positive control then becomes a
unit test: withhold the gold chunk, assert the Critic triggers; include it,
assert it does not.

### 6.6 An optional condition 4c

Korthals et al. use output diversity as an uncertainty signal. A cheap variant
of the Critic triggers re-retrieval when condition 3 samples disagree, instead
of when an LLM judges the evidence insufficient. If 4c matches 4, the
"insufficiency judgment" part of the mechanism is not what helps. This is
scope creep; only do it if 4 beats 3 and 4b and there is time to ask why.

### 6.7 Abstention and calibration analyses

`insufficient_evidence` is an abstention. Report coverage (fraction not
abstained) and accuracy on the covered set per condition, as a two-number
summary alongside macro-F1. The system already emits a confidence; a
reliability diagram per condition costs nothing and shows whether the Critic
improves calibration even where it does not improve F1.

### 6.8 Statistics details to fix now

Bootstrap resamples: 2,000 is standard. Paired bootstrap on the difference,
with the same resample indices for both conditions. Add McNemar on per-item
correctness as a second test. State the question-clustering caveat. Report
`insufficient_evidence` separately from the class-averaged F1 so abstaining
cannot game the metric.

### 6.9 Annotation acceleration without contaminating the eval

Pre-label with a model from a *different family* than the base model, then
correct by hand. Measure and report human-versus-pre-label kappa. Never use
the base model as pre-labeler, or the eval is biased toward it.

### 6.10 Cheap presentation deliverable

A static per-question report (misconception distribution, example evidence
spans) generated from `results/` by a script. It is the "instructor dashboard"
stretch goal at near-zero cost and makes the final presentation concrete.
